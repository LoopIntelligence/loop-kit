# Skills Discovery

Someone is asking what you can do. The wrong answer is a list of 130 skills.
The right answer is three things that would help *their* business this week.

## Before you answer

Read memory first. You need:
- what the business does
- who its customers are
- the frustration they named during onboarding
- what software they already pay for

If any of that is missing, ask one question to fill the biggest gap. One, not four.

## How to answer

Recommend **three skills. Never more.** For each one, three lines:

1. What it does, in plain words
2. Why it fits *their* business specifically — quote their own words back
3. Exactly what to type to try it

Then stop. Offer to go deeper if they want.

A good recommendation sounds like this:

> **Chasing unpaid invoices**
> I can go through what's outstanding and draft the follow-up emails.
> You said chasing money is the job you hate most and it eats your Fridays.
> Try: "Show me what's overdue and draft a chase-up for each one."

A bad recommendation sounds like this:

> **ar-collections-agent** — automated accounts receivable workflow management

## Matching guide

| What they told you | Point them at |
|---|---|
| Drowning in admin and paperwork | Automation, scheduled tasks, document building |
| Can't find new work | Outreach writing, lead research, competitor analysis |
| Quoting takes forever | Document building, branded PDFs, templates |
| Invoices go unpaid | Accounting connector, chase-up drafting |
| Too much time in email | Email connector, inbox triage, drafting |
| Compliance paperwork | Document building, forms, portal automation via browser |
| Don't know where AI fits | Start with the frustration they named — always |

## Two worth mentioning once they're going

**skill-creator** — when they catch themselves explaining the same process a
third time, you can turn it into a permanent skill. From then on you just know it.

**Scheduled tasks** — anything they do every Monday can run by itself.

Mention these at the end, in one line each. Don't lead with them.

## Tone

Keep the whole reply under fifteen lines. They asked a simple question.
If they want the full catalogue they'll ask for it, and then you can point
them at the skills list in the kit.
